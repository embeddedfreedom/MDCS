Engineering notes

Six write-ups from building MDCS

Each note is a problem that was actually hit during development, written in the same order it was encountered: the symptom, the diagnosis, the fix, and the limits of the fix. They are kept short on purpose. Nothing here is a tutorial, and nothing here claims more than was measured.

01 A 20 kHz integrator that heats but never coolsA thermal plant heated correctly but never cooled: at 32 °C the 50 µs step fell below half a float ULP, so every cooldown update rounded back to the previous state. 02 Fixed-rate loops on Linux, and what the jitter alert measuresWhy relative sleeps drift, how absolute CLOCK_MONOTONIC deadlines remove the drift, and what a jitter alert can and cannot tell you in a 20 kHz / 1 kHz loop. 03 A 12-byte HIL frame over USB CDCA fixed 12-byte host/MCU frame: sync hunting, the limits of an XOR checksum, a 3 ms poll deadline, and where control effort goes to zero when the board stops answering. 04 Porting a compensator to Cortex-M4 without doublesGetting a control law onto a Cortex-M4 with no libm and no doubles: the float-only rules, and the three linker checks that catch an accidental double. 05 What HIL validates, and what it does notWhat hardware-in-the-loop testing actually proves when the plant is simulated: a validated link and firmware, unvalidated physics, and no safety claim. 06 Lockstep HIL: the host owns the clockWhy the host owns the clock in a lockstep HIL rig: how a Linux preemption freezes plant and controller together, and the one condition that makes it valid.

Source of truth: docs/notes/ in the MDCS repository.
